Opportunities you may not have spotted

  • A’s “details open in place” and B’s “detail pane” are the same idea at two screen sizes. D uses one detail view that sits beside the list on a laptop and opens inside the list on a phone.
  • C’s guided flows don’t need their own screens. “Is it fixed?” is two buttons and a photo, so in E it opens inside the action’s row.
  • Experienced and new proprietors want different speeds for the same audit. E lets you choose Guided or List and remembers your choice. Every check still needs its own answer.
  • Multi-pharmacy can be a scope, not a separate page. D adds an “All / Harbourview / Kensal Street” switch to every page. If you only own one pharmacy, the side-by-side summary simply doesn’t appear.

2. D · Precision Command (A + B)

Problems it solves

  • B’s power felt heavy and dark.
  • A felt sparse on a laptop and hid everything behind single rows.
  • Owners of several pharmacies need to see across sites without a separate screen.

Borrowed

  • From A: white surfaces, hairline rows, dots with words for status, tabs, the month meter, details opening in place on a phone.
  • From B: the attention queue, the detail pane, the pharmacies side by side, ⌘K, J/K and 1–4 keys, the steps list beside the audit, the credentials matrix, the month grid.

Deliberately left out

  • B’s dark rail. The pharmacy switch moves into the header as a light scope control.
  • Coloured pills everywhere. Coloured words and dots carry status instead.
  • B’s three-column actions board. It doesn’t work on a phone, so it becomes grouped rows.
  • B’s “Snooze”. Postponing an obligation shouldn’t take one click, so “Plan a date” replaces it.
  • A’s opening greeting, as you asked.

New interactions

  • The scope switch on every page: All, or one pharmacy.
  • One detail view that’s a pane on a laptop and opens in the list on a phone.
  • “Why it’s here”: every queued item explains its reason in a sentence.
  • Calendar days you can select: picking a day shows its items. The unbooked site visit offers suggested dates.
  • The audit pane lists open actions in the same area, so you can close them while you’re on site.

Trade-offs

  • Busier than A: more columns and more on screen at once. It relies on strict ordering to stay calm.
  • On a phone, tables lose columns, and the matrices become lists with details opening in place.
  • Its full power (keys, ⌘K, the pane) is mainly a laptop benefit.

3. E · Guided Precision (A + C, with selected B)

Problems it solves

  • C sent you to a new screen for every job and hid the bigger picture behind “Records”.
  • A showed everything but didn’t help you do any of it.
  • Experienced and new proprietors need different paces.

Borrowed

  • From A: white surfaces, hairline lists, details opening in place, tabs, the month meter.
  • From C: the next-step card, the ordered “Then” path, the guided checks (fix, registration, verify, approve), Undo, the suggested plan.
  • From B, selectively: ⌘K, keys 1–4 in guided audits, a month mini-calendar.

Deliberately left out

  • C’s “Your path / Records” switch. E uses standard navigation, so every section is one tap away.
  • Separate full-screen flows for small jobs. They open in place instead.
  • B’s tables, matrices and side-by-side pharmacies. They would undo the calm, and E switches pharmacy instead.
  • C’s lavender background. Urgency colour is used only where it means something, as you asked.
  • Any “mark all satisfactory” shortcut. Speed must never become rubber-stamping.

New interactions

  • Guided / List for the site visit. List shows all six checks, each answered explicitly.
  • Guided checks inside the row for actions, staff and policies.
  • An Obligations column that stays visible on a laptop. On a phone it’s one tap away.
  • A next-step card with two entry points: “Start guided” or “Open as list”.

Trade-offs

  • On a phone, the full list of obligations is one tap away rather than always on screen.
  • Comparing pharmacies is weaker than in D: you switch between them rather than seeing them side by side.
  • The dark next-step card is the one heavy element. It earns its place by always being the most urgent job.
