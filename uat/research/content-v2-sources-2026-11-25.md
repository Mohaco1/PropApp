## My Health Records Rules 2026 (F2026L00392, registered 31 Mar 2026, commenced 1 Apr 2026)
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00392/asmade/text

**r 21(1): written policy.** A healthcare provider organisation must have a written security and access policy, auditable.

**r 21(2)(b): training** must be provided to a user:
- "(i) before the user is authorised to access the My Health Record system;"
- "(ii) annually;"
- "(iii) following any significant changes to the Act, regulations made under the Act, this instrument or the My Health Record system."

**r 21(2)(c): breach processes.** The policy covers "the processes that the organisation will use to ensure that the organisation does not contravene section 74 or 75 of the Act".

**r 21(4)(e): compromised accounts.** "suspending the access of a user as soon as practicable after becoming aware that the account or the password or other equivalent protection mechanism has been compromised". Under r 21(4)(f), the practices are reviewed "at least annually".

**r 43(4): policy versions.** "keep a record of each iteration of the policy for 5 years starting on the day the iteration comes into effect."

**r 44(1): 7 days.** "comply with a request from the System Operator under this section within 7 days of the organisation receiving the request."

**r 45: record-keeping.**
- What's recorded:
  - (a) applying the user authorisation and account procedures;
  - (b) training provided;
  - (c) applying the processes for s 74/75;
  - (d) applying the security measures.
- How long:
  - "(2) … (a) or (b) for 5 years";
  - "(3) … (c) or (d) for 2 years".
- Rule 45's example lists "records of the creation, modification, suspension and deactivation of user accounts" and "logs of individuals' access to the My Health Record system".

**r 72: transition.** For organisations registered before 1 Apr 2026, ss 42 and 44 of the My Health Records Rule 2016 "continue to apply … until immediately before 1 October 2026". Then "(3) Section 21 … applies to the entity on and after 1 October 2026."

## My Health Records Act 2012, s 75 (compilation 1 Jul 2026)
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2012A00063/latest/text

**s 75(1): when it applies.** It applies when a registered healthcare provider organisation "becomes aware" of any of these, and it directly involved, may have involved or may involve the entity:
- unauthorised collection, use or disclosure of My Health Record information;
- an event or circumstances that compromise (or may compromise) the security or integrity of the system.

**s 75(2): who to notify.** "as soon as practicable after becoming aware, the entity must notify … (d) otherwise — both the System Operator and the Information Commissioner." Civil penalty: 1,500 penalty units.

**s 75(5): if it may have happened.**
- "(a) so far as is reasonably practicable contain…";
- "(b) evaluate any risks…";
- "(c) if … the effects … might be serious for at least one healthcare recipient: (i) … ask the System Operator to notify all healthcare recipients that would be affected".

**s 75(6): if it has happened.**
- Contain it and do a preliminary assessment of the causes.
- Evaluate the risks.
- Ask the System Operator to notify all affected healthcare recipients (and the public if a significant number are affected).
- Take steps to prevent or mitigate further breaches.

## Privacy Act 1988, Part IIIC (compilation 4 Jun 2026)
URL: https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text

- **s 26WH: assess.** "carry out a reasonable and expeditious assessment … completed within 30 days after the entity becomes aware".
- **s 26WK: statement.** Prepare a statement and "give a copy of the statement to the Commissioner … as soon as practicable".
- **s 26WL: tell people.** "notify the contents of the statement to each of the individuals" (to whom it relates, or who are at risk). If that's not practicable, publish it.

## NSW Health: Initiation of the oral contraceptive pill (current as at 24 Aug 2026)
URL: https://www.health.nsw.gov.au/pharmaceutical/pharmacists/Pages/oral-contraceptive-pill-initiation.aspx

**The service.** "Eligible NSW pharmacists can initiate, change or resupply the oral contraceptive pill (OCP) for eligible low-risk women in the community pharmacy setting." Pharmacies must have appropriate facilities and ensure "participating pharmacists have completed approved university-level training through James Cook University".

**The pharmacist must:**
- hold a Graduate Certificate of Advanced Practice and Prescribing for Pharmacists (JCU);
- submit the Intention form (and resubmit if details change);
- keep records on an approved secure digital platform: 2 years under the Regulation, generally 7 years under privacy and health records law;
- share a record with the GP, with consent, within 7 days;
- consult in a private space;
- keep the healthdirect listing current.

**Patients:** female, aged 18–39, low risk.

**Consent:** the patient is told the consultation fee, that medicines are at private (non-PBS) cost with no Safety Net, and about any tests.

**Pilot:** "fund the first 5,000 eligible consultations ($60 … initiation … $32 per review consultation)".

**Note:** the page cites "clause 48B of the Poisons and Therapeutic Goods Regulation 2008". It predates the 5 Nov 2026 Regulation, and the 20 Nov FAQ doesn't mention it. The app says to check the current NSW Health page.
