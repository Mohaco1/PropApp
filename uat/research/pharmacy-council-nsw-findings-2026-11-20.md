# Pharmacy Council of NSW (hpca.nsw.gov.au/councils/pharmacy), read 20 Nov 2026

## Proprietor responsibilities (PBA Guidelines for proprietor pharmacists, quoted by the Council)
- Every financial-interest holder, from 1% to 100%, must:
  - maintain, and be able to demonstrate, awareness of how the business is conducted;
  - intervene where needed.
- These obligations can't be delegated. No "silent partner"; interstate owners must still know NSW law.
- Oversight is shown by:
  - interacting with co-proprietors and staff;
  - policies and procedures for all services, with staff trained;
  - regular on-site visits;
  - regular audits and inspections of all aspects of the business.
- The Guidelines can be used in disciplinary proceedings (National Law s 41).
- Case studies:
  - An owner relied on the franchise group and visited every 2–3 months; thousands of S8 went missing. Conditions were imposed.
  - An owner rarely visited; prescriptions from a prohibited GP were dispensed and stock checks missed. Registration was suspended.
- **App relevance:** this is exactly what the app is for. It records the owner's oversight: visits, reviews, procedures and staff training.

## Notify the Council when
- a fit-out or renovation of the dispensary and surrounds is completed (inspector notified);
- the pharmacy name changes;
- ownership changes (a partner added or removed);
- a financial interest is acquired or disposed of;
- the pharmacy closes or relocates;
- a medication storage and preparation room is added or changed (forms exist for each);
- a collection unit is installed (NSW Health exemption condition).

## Annual cycle
- Premises registration renewal: notice in May, fee due by 30 June; approval runs to 30 June the next year.
- Annual financial-interest declaration: covers 1 May–30 April, due by 30 June.
- At renewal, the Council recommends reviewing policies and systems against The Guide and the PBA proprietor guidelines.

## Routine inspections (article, 15 Apr 2026)
- The premises standards are in the Health Practitioner Regulation (NSW) Regulation 2025: required equipment, publications and anything else needed for safe services.
- Most common failures:
  - current Therapeutic Guidelines (digital) and Australian Medicines Handbook not readily accessible;
  - no hot and cold water sink for the dispensary.

## "Pharmacy owners: what to include in regular business audits" (Council guideline, 18 Mar 2024)
Some details predate 5 Nov 2026 (balance check was March/Sept, now 3-monthly; outstanding phone scripts was 7 days, now 14).
- **S3:**
  - storage;
  - labels show the pharmacy name and contacts;
  - pseudoephedrine recorded in Project STOP (NSW Health now says no Regulation record is needed; Project STOP is still industry practice);
  - written S3 supply procedure known to staff;
  - list of misusable S3 (e.g. Rikodeine, Restavit), with staff aware and orders checked against sales.
- **S4:**
  - S4D balance checks, with losses reported;
  - AAS scripts lawful;
  - special-authority scripts from authorised prescribers;
  - barcode scanning to reduce errors.
- **S8:**
  - balance checks, with losses reported;
  - scripts lawful;
  - quantity and purpose appropriate;
  - psychostimulant approvals (no longer needed on the script from 5 Nov 2026);
  - only pharmacists hold the safe key.
- **OTP:** self-audit every 6–12 months for at least 15% of OTP patients.
- **Prescription management:** no "owing scripts"; emergency 7-day supply and PBS continued dispensing only.
- **DAA:** dispensing and packing protocol; QCPP 2020 sub-domain 5; PPA rules; PSA DAA guidelines.
- **Vaccination:** NSW Standards; documentation (screening/consent, AIR).

## The Guide (last updated 1 May 2021; still cites the old 1966 Act, so verify against Health Practitioner Regulation (NSW) 2025 Sch 5)
- **Premises equipment (Sch 5):**
  - purpose-built vaccine fridge;
  - dispensing balance;
  - scales to 1 kg (with weights) or electronic ≤50 mg increments;
  - measures of 200, 100, 10 and 5 mL;
  - funnel;
  - 2 mortars and pestles (1 glass);
  - stirring rod;
  - 2 spatulas;
  - ointment slab;
  - tablet counting tray.
  - Balances, scales and measures stamped (National Measurement Act).
- **Publications:**
  - the Act and Regulation (now Medicines, Poisons and Therapeutic Goods);
  - the Poisons List or the Pharmacy Guild NSW "Guide to the NSW Medicines and Poisons Schedules";
  - the National Law and the Regulation;
  - the Price Information Code of Practice;
  - the PBA Guideline 1 reference texts (current Therapeutic Guidelines and AMH are the most common failures).
- **Dispensary (cl 12(2)):**
  - adequately lit and ventilated;
  - heating;
  - stainless or impervious sink with impervious surround and HOT AND COLD water;
  - bench ≥40 cm wide with ≥1 m² free working space and impervious cover (free space, not machinery tops);
  - at least one barcode scanner per dispensing station.
- Infection control standards: Sch 3 of the Regulation.
- **Inspections:**
  - Council inspectors are authorised persons;
  - obstructing them is an offence;
  - routine compliance inspection EVERY 18 MONTHS;
  - inspection before opening, relocation or substantial renovation;
  - all pharmacists must be able to show their current registration.
  - Inspectors can destroy unusable S8.
- Closure: Notice of closure within 14 DAYS.
- Online pharmacy: only attached to approved premises.
- **Offences / unsatisfactory conduct (Guide Note 24):**
  - no current approval;
  - unregistered financial-interest holders;
  - failing to notify cessation of an interest;
  - non-pharmacist owners;
  - more than 5 pharmacies;
  - no pharmacist in charge personally supervising;
  - annual declaration not lodged on time;
  - **owner name(s) not displayed at or near the main entrance;**
  - **pharmacist-in-charge name not displayed next to the dispensing area;**
  - prices breaching the Price Information Code of Practice.

## Supporting resources for proprietor oversight (30 Mar 2023)
- Procedures for every employee type: pharmacists, graduates, locums, technicians, assistants, delivery drivers, contractors.
- Barcode scan immediately before labelling.
- Tasks can be delegated, but overall responsibility can't. Monitor completion and review staff activity.
- **Oversight methods:**
  - on-site visits;
  - checklists;
  - audits;
  - observing practice;
  - staff meetings;
  - employee check-ins;
  - reporting systems.
- **Schedule weekly, monthly and annually:**
  - stock check (including S8);
  - high-risk medicines;
  - owing reports;
  - uncollected script report;
  - negative stock;
  - wholesalers.
- **Incidents:** identify near misses, review protocols, document and reflect, improve. Call professional indemnity (PDL 1300 854 838).
## Vaccination checklist (Council)
- CPR yearly AND first aid certificate every 3 YEARS (no grace period).
- COVID and JE modules before giving those vaccines.
- First aid couch (or similar).
- AIR within 24 h (the Standards say preferably 24 h, at most 10 business days).
- GuildCare and MedAdvisor are compliant recording systems.
## DAA advice (Council)
- Don't pack medicines not in original containers, or with illegible or missing expiry.
- A DAA is a clinical service.
- PSA Standards 2, 4, 9 and 12; PSA DAA guidelines.
- PDL practice alerts.
## Fraudulent prescriptions (Council, 18 Mar 2024)
- **Red flags:**
  - someone phones ahead to check stock;
  - verbal, fax or email scripts with letters attached;
  - non-standard form;
  - no or bad eRx barcode;
  - no SafeScript prescribing record;
  - large or PBS-exceeding quantities;
  - patient or prescriber outside the area;
  - after-hours urgency.
- **Actions:**
  - copy the script first;
  - verify independently;
  - document the steps;
  - report on the online form;
  - **notifying NSW Police is a legislative requirement.**
## Mandatory notifications (Ahpra)
- Employers and all registered practitioners must notify Ahpra of notifiable conduct: impairment, intoxication, significant departure from standards, sexual misconduct.
