What goes wrong, and the rule that fixes it

1

Half the screen goes before the first question

Every review form opens with the pharmacy name, the same "Proprietor oversight. Refer to operational records…" paragraph, a second heading that repeats it, then the legal text. On average the first question starts halfway down the phone, after about 48 words. On the S8 check it is 149 words and the bottom edge; on the OTP comprehensive review it is below the first screen.

Rule: The first thing to do is visible on the first screen. The legal basis is one line with "Why?" to open the rest.

2

The same thing said twice

24 of 32 review forms show the save message twice ("Drafts save as you type", then "Draft saves as you type"). "2 need a date" appears twice on one setup row. Meetings and actions repeat "Action ·" on every row.

Rule: Say it once. One saved indicator, always in the same place.

3

Edges that do not line up

In 36 of 61 screens, headings and text start at different edges. Review titles run to the screen edge while the text beneath is indented.

Rule: One page shell with one side margin for every screen.

4

Two ways to answer one question

Several reviews show a "Checked" option and a "Reviewed · satisfactory" tick box for the same question.

Rule: One control per question.

5

Two different review windows

Some reviews use Back, a progress bar and a stepper. Others (OTP monthly, meetings) use Close and one long form. The same job looks different depending on which review it is.

Rule: One review shell for everything.

6

Words that go against your decisions

"Responsible person or role" on every action. "Attendees or pharmacist in charge consulted". "Responsible staff". "Review proprietor oversight and responsibilities". Procedures open with "Responsibilities".

Rule: Proprietor-only words: no "responsible person" and no pharmacist-in-charge fields. The proprietor is responsible, whoever is on duty.

7

A big black button for the wrong job

List pages open with search, then a full-width black "Add …" button, before any content: Add action, Add policy, Add meeting, Schedule meeting, Adjust intervals. Adding is rare; reading the list is the job.

Rule: The main button is the main job of that screen. Adding goes in a small + button.

8

Tabs cut off at the side

Filter tabs run off the right edge with no sign there is more.

Rule: Nothing is cut off. Tabs wrap, or scroll with a visible cue.

9

Counts that disagree or read wrongly

Today: 8 overdue. Actions: 5. Calendar: "8 overdue" above "Overdue · 9". Other wording: "1 reviews overdue", "0 setup incomplete", "Credentials 0" while checks are recorded.

Rule: One set of counts from one place, the same on every screen, with correct singular and plural.

10

Dates in four styles

"21 Aug", "2026-08-21", "21/08/2026, 12:00 am", and "updated 27 Nov" for a date last year. Midnight times shown as if real.

Rule: One date style: "21 Aug" this year, "21 Aug 2025" otherwise. Times only when they mean something.

11

Walls of text and fields

The annual staff check is 4.9 screens of dropdowns. A procedure is 4.5 screens before its first button. History is 6.7 screens. The staff profile opens with a long list of suggestions.

Rule: At most one screen of reading before you can act. Long forms become steps or a quick confirm list.

12

Today shows the wrong things first

Four rows for one person. Two rows for one S8 check. A legal item below the fold.

Rule: Group by person and by obligation. "Required by law" first.

13

Windows that do not fit a phone

The action window leaves a grey strip on the right. Buttons touch the heading below them. The "Saved" message covers content in setup.

Rule: Windows fill the phone. Consistent spacing between blocks. Messages never cover what you are doing.

14

Small targets everywhere

686 buttons or links under 44 px tall across 61 screens. The search button is 44×18 px.

Rule: Every tap target at least 44 px.

Design rules for the rebuild

Every new screen is checked against these before you see it.

  1. First screen, first jobThe first thing to do is visible without scrolling on a 390 × 844 phone. At most one screen of reading before you can act.
  2. Say it onceNo sentence twice on a screen. One saved indicator, in one place. The pharmacy name only in the header.
  3. Law in one lineEach review shows its legal basis in one line ("Every 3 months · required by law, s 111"). The full wording sits under "Why?", word for word. Warnings that matter get a short amber note.
  4. One shellEvery review, form and window uses the same frame: back, title, saved state, the same side margins, full phone width.
  5. One way to answerOne control per question. Answers are big rows you tap.
  6. Proprietor wordsNo "responsible person" and no pharmacist-in-charge fields. The proprietor is responsible, whoever is on duty.
  7. The main job gets the main buttonOne black button per screen, for that screen's main job. "Add" is a small + button.
  8. One set of countsEvery count comes from one place, so Today, Reviews, Actions and Calendar always agree. Correct singular and plural.
  9. One date style"21 Aug" this year, "21 Aug 2025" otherwise. No times unless they mean something.
  10. Today by person and dutyOne row per person and one per obligation. "Required by law" comes first.
  11. Built for thumbsEvery tap target at least 44 px. Nothing cut off, no sideways scroll, tabs never hidden.
  12. Short formsLong forms become steps or a quick confirm list ("All still current?", then change only what has changed).
  13. Checked every buildEach build ends with a page of phone screenshots. Automated checks for rules 1, 2, 8 and 11. Tests tap real buttons and fail on any console error.
